\section{Recommendation Register}
\label{app:recommendation-register}

Section~\ref{sec:risk-rubric} requires every recommendation record to identify
the findings addressed, the enforcing actor or owner, response urgency,
dependencies, effort, sequence and target horizon, acceptance evidence,
status, and residual-risk decision. This appendix is that register. It
consolidates the forty-three recommendations issued across
Sections~\ref{sec:core-lib-format}--\ref{sec:supply-chain} into a single
reverse index from recommendation to finding, owner, work package, and
observable acceptance evidence.

The register does not create, revise, or imply a risk rating.
\textbf{Recommendation identifiers name items only.} Their numbers express no
risk, urgency, effort, dependency, or delivery order, and a recommendation
addressing several findings does not thereby acquire a tier. Where a
\emph{route} is shown, it is the maximum inherited response route of the
findings the recommendation addresses, reproduced from
Table~\ref{tab:findings-response-routing} for convenience; it is a property of
those findings, not of the recommendation. A recommendation whose only linked
record is an unrated evidence record inherits no urgency and is marked
\emph{Evidence-conditional}.

Dependencies, delivery sequence, and target horizons are maintained in
Appendix~\ref{app:phased-roadmap} through the work-package column; they are
not duplicated here. Effort uses the definitions in
Table~\ref{tab:treatment-planning-fields} and describes work breadth, not
elapsed time or risk.

\paragraph{Effort revised against existing work.}
Six recommendations --- \code{CORE-S1}, \code{CORE-S2}, \code{CORE-S4},
\code{CORE-S5}, \code{CORE-S6}, and \code{APP-S2} --- describe controls that
are already designed, and in several cases built and measured, in the H5Lens /
\code{hdf5-pickles} project. Their effort below is the \emph{revised} estimate
from Table~\ref{tab:core-prior-art-rescope}, which assumes reuse of existing
identifiers, boundaries, fixtures, and measurements as data, subject to the
licensing boundary in Section~\ref{sec:roadmap-licensing}. Each is marked
\textbf{(re-scoped)}. Effort is still not subtracted from risk, and a smaller
estimate never shortens a response clock.

\paragraph{Status and residual-risk decision.}
At publication every recommendation in this register has status
\emph{Proposed}, implementation status \emph{not verified}, and \textbf{no
recorded residual-risk decision}. These two fields are deliberately not
tabulated per row in this revision, because populating them with a uniform
value would misrepresent an unadopted plan as a tracked program. On adoption,
the roadmap authority should add the per-row status vocabulary and
residual-risk disposition required by
Appendix~\ref{app:phased-roadmap}.

\subsection{Correspondence with SSP SIG Policy Controls}
\label{sec:rec-ssp-crosswalk}

The SSP SIG maintains a catalog of \code{HDF5-SHINES-*} policy
controls~\cite{hdf5-ssp-controls}, which is a \emph{different namespace} from
the recommendation identifiers in this appendix
(Section~\ref{sec:identifier-scheme}). The correspondence below is recorded so
that the two documents can be read together, and so that a reader of the
OpenSSF coverage matrix~\cite{hdf5-ssp-osps-matrix} can see which of this
report's recommendations bear on a control family that matrix scores.

\textbf{The mapping is stated at family granularity, not control granularity},
because the control catalog was consulted through its published references
rather than enumerated in full for this revision; a control-level mapping is an
adoption-time task for the \code{GOV} role. \textbf{A correspondence is not a
claim that the control is satisfied.} A recommendation may bear on a control
and, if implemented, still leave it unmet --- and the SSP SIG, not this report,
renders that judgment.

\subsection{Recommendations Not Tied to a Formal Finding}

Three recommendation groups deliberately address no rated finding, and this is
recorded rather than hidden:

\begin{description}
    \item[\code{APP-S7}] is a triage-process control. It improves the
          applicability signal that the \code{RM-1A} census and \code{RM-4A}
          reassessment consume, but it treats no adverse-event scenario and
          therefore inherits no urgency.
    \item[The extension track] (\code{VOL-S1}--\code{VOL-S4},
          \code{FILTER-S1}--\code{FILTER-S5}, \code{VFD-S1}--\code{VFD-S4})
          and \code{CORE-PRIV-S2} address the unrated evidence record
          \code{EXT-PRIV-001}. They are proactive assurance proposals. Their
          existence does not convert that observation into a rated finding,
          and their delivery does not by itself establish residual-risk
          reduction.
    \item[\code{CORE-S11}, \code{APP-S6}, and \code{SUP-S1}] address the
          unrated evidence record \code{SUP-SAFE-001}. They also supply
          provenance inputs used across the register, but provenance is not
          treatment.
\end{description}

In an earlier revision one finding had no recommendation at all:
\code{CORE-SAFE-001} was recorded as a treatment gap. That gap is closed ---
\code{CORE-SAFE-S1} now addresses it, drawing on treatment options that had
already been proposed publicly by maintainers but had not been carried into
this report. \textbf{Every registered finding now has at least one linked
recommendation.}
