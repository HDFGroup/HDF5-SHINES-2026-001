\section{Phased Mitigation Roadmap}
\label{app:phased-roadmap}

This appendix converts the response routing in
Table~\ref{tab:findings-response-routing} into a proposed delivery plan. It is
an assessment recommendation, not evidence that an organization has accepted
the dates, assigned the work, or implemented the controls. The response clocks
in Phases~0--2 begin when an affected deployment or equivalent exposure is
identified, as specified in Table~\ref{tab:findings-response-routing}; the
scope census in \code{RM-1A}, assert-site catalog in \code{RM-1E}, and the
Phase~3 program horizon begin when the roadmap is adopted. The assessment
sponsor should record a dated decision to adopt, modify, or reject the
proposal; adoption must set due dates for both inventories so a response clock
cannot remain latent through unbounded non-discovery. A pre-adoption exposure
still enters the response route and is not held for the program decision.

The roadmap does not revise inherent risk. Phase, dependency, owner, effort,
and implementation order are treatment-planning fields under
Table~\ref{tab:treatment-planning-fields}; none is added to or subtracted from
a finding's impact, exposure, tier, or confidence. A dependency that prevents
a final control from shipping does not extend a response horizon: the
accountable owner must maintain an interim containment, record the variance,
and obtain an explicit residual-risk decision.

At publication, every work package below has planning status
\emph{Proposed} and implementation status \emph{not verified}. The role labels
are proposed accountability boundaries, not assertions about present HDF Group
or downstream assignments. No residual-risk acceptance is recorded by this
roadmap. Within ten business days of adoption, the roadmap authority should
replace each role label with a named accountable person, issue or program
record, dated target or release, and implementation status. For a triggered
Phase~0 record, the \code{APP} owner and \code{GOV} risk authority are assigned
at T0 and containment begins immediately; the ten-day allowance applies only
to untriggered program packages.

One role is accountable for each work package even when several roles perform
the work. The named accountable person may delegate tasks but remains
responsible for the evidence package and readiness attestation; only
\code{GOV} approves a program gate, exception, or residual-risk disposition. A
package that
spans independently governed projects or deployments must be instantiated once
per accountable owner and scope; the generic \code{APP} package, for example,
does not make one downstream responsible for another. Every instance also
names its responsible implementer, an \code{ASSURE} verifier independent of
that implementer, and a separate release or deployment approver that authorizes
rollout after verification. For a High or Critical scope, the verifier may not
also be the package owner, rollout approver, or risk acceptor.

\subsection{Phase Gates and Clocks}

Phases are delivery waves, not severity levels. Work may proceed in parallel,
and a later-phase structural control may begin before an earlier phase closes.
The earlier response clock still controls. Phase~2 also contains evidence work
for unrated records; its placement is an assurance target and does not assign
those records a response urgency.

When a package covers findings with different response routes, each finding
retains its own maximum horizon. Co-delivering a Planned item during Phase~1 is
an implementation convenience, not an increase in its risk or urgency; a
Planned item in the same package cannot be used to defer a Near-term item to the
later clock.

Each finding-and-scope instance therefore has an SLA child record separate from
the work package's final-control or structural due date. The child records the
finding ID, defined census boundary and completeness attestation, deployment or
artifact scope, T0, urgency and maximum horizon, required interim action, due
date, performed date, status, and evidence. A 24-hour containment can close its
SLA child without falsely marking a shared architectural package complete; a
large shared package cannot hide an overdue child behind its later program date.

\subsection{Licensing Constraint on Reuse of Existing Work}
\label{sec:roadmap-licensing}

Several packages below --- \code{RM-1E}, \code{RM-2A}, \code{RM-2C},
\code{RM-3A}, \code{RM-3B}, and \code{RM-3C} --- may be able to draw on
reviewed work that already
exists in the H5Lens / \code{hdf5-pickles} project, which supplies a bounded
decoder, a machine-readable invariant registry, an untrusted-input preflight
oracle, a malformed-input corpus, and differential measurements against a
selected libhdf5 build~\cite{hdf5-pickles,h5policy,h5policy-invariant-registry}.
Section~\ref{sec:core-prior-art} describes what exists and how each
recommendation is re-scoped as a result.

The projects report different licenses: \textbf{H5Lens is distributed under
the GNU General Public License, version 3 or later, while the HDF5 library is
distributed under a permissive BSD-style license.} That difference is an
implementation, provenance, and governance constraint to resolve before reuse;
it is not a legal conclusion by this assessment. This report does not determine
whether particular identifiers, fixtures, outputs, source-derived materials,
linking arrangements, or redistributions are copyrightable, compatible,
derivative, or permitted. Before implementation, the accountable
\code{GOV}/\code{REL} roles should inventory the relevant licenses and rights,
record provenance for each proposed input, and obtain qualified license or
legal review where needed.

For planning only, the roadmap assumes that separately reviewed factual
interfaces, test inputs, and measurement outputs may be usable as evidence for
an independently implemented native control. That assumption must be confirmed
for each artifact and does not authorize source transfer, linking, vendoring,
or redistribution. Subject to that review, the proposed implementation pattern
is:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,style=nextline]
    \item[Review as candidate data; implement independently.]
    \code{RM-3A} may evaluate existing invariant identifiers, record-family
    decomposition, and structural boundaries as candidate registry vocabulary
    after provenance and license review, while writing native enforcement code
    independently. The registry is planning and specification input; it is not
    assumed to authorize source reuse or patches.

    \item[Evaluate external-oracle use separately from component reuse.]
    \code{RM-2A} and \code{RM-3C} may propose running the existing preflight
    oracle as a separate differential reference in CI --- comparing what
    libhdf5 does against what the oracle decides --- without assuming that
    linking, vendoring, packaging, or redistribution is permitted. The exact
    invocation, data exchange, distribution model, and resulting artifacts
    require the review described above.

    \item[Review fixtures and expectations artifact by artifact.]
    \code{RM-1B}, \code{RM-2C}, and \code{RM-3C} may evaluate malformed
    specimens and expected-outcome records as corpus inputs. Each specimen
    needs its own provenance and license check: some are
    generator-produced and reproducible, and others are supplied corpus finds
    whose redistribution terms must be confirmed before they enter a public
    test suite.

    \item[Case records are triage input, not findings.]
    \code{RM-1E} may take the existing case records as its starting inventory.
    They are separate project artifacts, not independent third-party
    validation; each still requires this programme's own reachability,
    version-boundary, and consequence determination before it is promoted and
    routed.
\end{description}

The accountable \code{GOV} role should record a dated implementation decision
after the review above. The present effort estimates assume that reviewed
identifiers, fixtures, and measurements can reduce some design or enumeration
work while native implementation and verification remain necessary. If that
assumption is not approved, the affected package estimates must be revisited.
Any proposal to relicense, dual-license, link, vendor, distribute, or upstream
existing material requires a separate recorded decision based on qualified
review and is not assumed by this roadmap.

\subsection{Minimum Viable Subset Under Constrained Capacity}
\label{sec:roadmap-minimum-viable}

The register above is scoped to risk, not to budget. It proposes multiple
\emph{Large} packages, several of which are multi-release architectural
programs. A plan that cannot be started is not a control, so this subsection
identifies what to fund first if the full program cannot be resourced.

\paragraph{What this subsection is and is not.}
This is a funding and sequencing aid. It is \textbf{not} a risk rank, and it
does not revise any inherent rating, response route, or maximum horizon.
Section~\ref{sec:risk-rubric} forbids effort from lowering urgency, and
nothing here does so: Tier~A below is the Phase~0--1 work whose omission would
breach an Immediate or Near-term response clock, and it is therefore not
discretionary at any funding level. The bounded Planned-route deliverables in
\code{RM-2C} and \code{RM-2H} likewise retain their 90-day clocks; only their
broader structural follow-on may be capacity-sequenced. Tiers~B and C order the
remaining \emph{prevention} program, which has program commitments rather than
finding-response clocks. Deferring a Tier~C item is permissible only with the
recorded, expiring exception required by
Section~\ref{sec:roadmap-change-control}, and each deferral carries the
consequence stated in Table~\ref{tab:roadmap-minimum-viable}.

\paragraph{Tier A --- not discretionary.}
\code{RM-0A} (when triggered), \code{RM-1A}, \code{RM-1B}, \code{RM-1C},
\code{RM-1D}, \code{RM-1E}, and \code{RM-1F}. Every one is \emph{Medium}
effort. Together they are the Phase~0--1 response obligation: contain a
confirmed Critical-equivalent path, establish the population, treat the known
affected scope, place identified write-side deployments under operational
containment, and bound the assert-masking perimeter. \code{RM-1A} is first
among equals for scoping --- without a census, the program cannot demonstrate
population coverage, and the version-and-path gap recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:cve-history-summary} cannot be closed.

\paragraph{Bounded Phase 2 obligations.}
The case-specific treatment in \code{RM-2C} and the archival-population
disposition in \code{RM-2H} complete on their Planned 90-day clocks. They are
not displaced by the prevention ordering below. The broader compatibility
matrix, machine-readable format work, automated dependency reporting, and
archival-profile design remain separately tracked structural work.

\paragraph{Tier B --- assessor-selected prevention sequence.}
The following five items were selected because each is independently useful
and, in the assessor's qualitative judgment, existing artifacts reduce some
implementation work. No measured engineer-week, monetary cost, or comparative
control-effectiveness model was available; this is a proposed sequence under
the stated reuse and effort assumptions, not a cost-efficiency finding. The
ordering reflects the effort revised in
Table~\ref{tab:core-prior-art-rescope}: because the
invariant registry, the profile semantics, the corpus, and the fuzz-harness
design already exist and, subject to the artifact-level review in
Section~\ref{sec:roadmap-licensing}, may reduce implementation work, four of
the five are estimated as Small or Medium rather than Large. \code{CORE-S1} in
particular moves up under that assumption: adapting an approved existing
invariant vocabulary and mapping it to enforcement points is estimated as less
work than designing the vocabulary from nothing, and it is a dependency for the
rest of the registry-driven work.

\paragraph{Tier C --- defer explicitly, with the consequence recorded.}
The remaining Large programs. Deferring them is a legitimate capacity
decision; representing them as unnecessary is not.

\subsection{Change Control and Residual Risk}
\label{sec:roadmap-change-control}

Each implementation record should contain the package identifier, named
accountable person, responsible implementer, independent verifier, release or
deployment approver, affected scope, linked findings and recommendations,
linked finding-and-scope SLA child records, trigger event, trigger owner, actual
trigger date, recommended maximum horizon, approved committed date or release,
hard and coordination dependencies with
their owner and state, effort estimate and assumptions, status, evidence links,
target residual tier and status with its scope and assumptions, named risk-
tolerance authority, exception identifier, reassessment trigger, and the last
decision date. Status
should use
\emph{Proposed}, \emph{Accepted}, \emph{In progress}, \emph{Blocked},
\emph{Implemented pending verification}, \emph{Verified},
\emph{Deferred under exception}, \emph{Risk accepted}, or
\emph{Superseded}. A missed due-date event is recorded immutably as
\emph{Overdue}; a later active exception may add \emph{Deferred under
exception} for the exact scope but does not erase the miss. Blocked, deferred,
accepted, or superseded work requires an explanatory decision record.

Any horizon, gate, control, compatibility, or residual-risk exception requires
a named \code{GOV} approver, rationale, affected scope and unmet requirement,
interim or compensating controls with evidence, monitoring owner and cadence,
issue date, expiration date, and automatic reopen or reassessment triggers.
Exceptions do not revise inherent risk and cannot be indefinite. No exception
permits a known, uncontained Critical-equivalent external-resource path to
continue untrusted processing: the path is contained or the job is stopped;
an exception may govern only later treatment or architecture.

After acceptance testing, the implementer supplies the artifacts,
\code{ASSURE} verifies them, the package owner attests readiness, and the
release or deployment approver authorizes rollout. The named \code{GOV} risk
authority then reassesses current residual risk using
Section~\ref{sec:risk-rubric}, identifies the controls actually credited, and
records a decision to accept, further reduce, avoid, or transfer the remaining
risk. Target residual risk is not marked achieved before that review, and the
verified current residual is recorded as the later result.
Each reassessment is a dated, immutable snapshot with its scope, assumptions,
verifier, and evidence; it does not overwrite the original inherent assessment.

The roadmap authority should review Phases~0--2 at least weekly while open,
Phase~3 at each planned release gate, and Phase~4 at its stated cadence. New
findings enter the response route first and are then mapped to the earliest
work package capable of meeting that route; they are not assigned a phase from
recommendation numbering or implementation convenience.
Event-driven reassessment is also required after a control failure, new
incident or reproducer, materially changed HDF5 or downstream release/default,
changed trust boundary, privilege, automation, data sensitivity or dependency,
fuzz or telemetry regression, and exception expiry.
For recurring \code{RM-4A} work, each review produces a dated point-in-time
verification snapshot rather than a permanent completion claim. A later gate
failure, event trigger, or affected release reopens the scoped record and
creates a new SLA child and reassessment snapshot while preserving the prior
history.
